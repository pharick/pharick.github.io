In August 2018 I worked through the learn.javascript.ru tutorial (the Russian
javascript.info) and kept every exercise: 45 chapters on the language in `lang/`
and 20 on the DOM, events and UI in `document/`. Each task sits in its own
folder as a Node script or a browser page, and 29 of them come with mocha specs
written against `assert` or `chai`.

The language part runs from loops and recursion through closures, decorators,
`bind`/currying and prototypes to ES2015 classes and promises. The pieces I
still find useful are the timing helpers - a `throttle` that remembers the last
suppressed call and replays it when the cooldown ends - and the array work, such
as Kadane's maximal subarray with an eight-case spec.

The browser part is where the code gets larger. The slider is a class that takes
`{min, max, initVal}` and an `onChange` callback, attaches `mousemove` to the
document on `mousedown` and clamps the thumb to the track. The hover-intent
widget samples pointer speed on an interval and only fires when the pointer
slows down, filtering `mouseout` by `relatedTarget`. The sortable table handles
one click on the whole table, detaches `tbody`, sorts rows by the header's
`data-type` and reattaches it. There are also polyfills for `matches`, `closest`
and `remove`, a calendar-table generator and a tooltip driven by `data-tooltip`
attributes.

The style follows the tutorial's pre-ES2015 edition - `var`, function
expressions, prototype chains - with `let`, classes and arrow functions
appearing in the later chapters. Comments and test names are in Russian, there
is no `package.json`, and a few exercises were left unfinished. I keep it as a
record of learning the platform.
