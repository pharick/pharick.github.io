matrix is my solution to École 42's *Enter the Matrix*: a header-only C++20
library with dynamically sized `Vector<T>` and `Matrix<T>` covering the
subject's fifteen exercises, from element-wise arithmetic and linear
combinations through reduced row echelon form, determinant, inverse, rank and an
OpenGL-style perspective projection.

The design decision I am most satisfied with is separating views from ownership.
`VectorView<T>` is a pointer-plus-size that carries all the arithmetic;
`Vector<T>` derives from it and owns a heap array. `Matrix<T>::operator[]`
returns a `VectorView` over a column with no copy, so expressions like `m[i] =
m[j] * 2` or `m[i] += v` write straight into the matrix storage, and the matrix
algorithms are written in terms of the vector operations rather than duplicating
them. Storage is column-major so the projection matrix can be handed to OpenGL
directly, while nested initializer lists are still written the way a matrix is
read.

The subject restricts the numerics: no `std::sqrt`, and fused multiply-add
accumulation. Dot products, norms and linear combinations therefore go through
`std::fma`, and the Euclidean norm uses `pow(x, 0.5)`. Row reduction is
Gauss–Jordan with partial pivoting on the largest-magnitude entry; determinant
and inverse use cofactor expansion and the adjugate, which is adequate for the
2×2 to 4×4 cases the subject targets. `lerp` is one generic template that works
for scalars, vectors and matrices because the types share `+`, `-` and scalar
`*`. Both classes follow the rule of five with copy-and-swap, and size
mismatches throw `std::invalid_argument`.

There are 73 Catch2 test cases across four files, covering construction and
copy/move semantics, aliasing through views, every operator, the error paths and
the subject's worked examples.

Limitations: cofactor expansion is exponential, so determinant and inverse are
meant for the small matrices the subject targets, and the column index is not
bounds-checked.
