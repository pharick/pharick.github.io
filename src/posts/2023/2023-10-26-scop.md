scop is my École 42 OpenGL project: a viewer that loads a Wavefront .obj file,
normalises the model to a unit cube and renders it with OpenGL 3.3 core. The
subject allows a windowing library and nothing else, so GLFW and GLEW are the
only dependencies; the matrices, quaternions, OBJ parser and BMP decoder are
written by hand in C++11.

The OBJ parser strips comments, tokenises lines, reads v and f records and fan-
triangulates polygons; v/vt/vn indices are handled by reading the leading vertex
index. I track the bounding box while parsing so the model can be translated to
the origin and scaled to a 2-unit cube whatever the file's units. The BMP reader
maps the file headers onto packed structs, checks the BM magic and 24-bit depth,
seeks to the pixel offset and converts BGR to RGB before uploading with mipmaps.

For transforms I wrote a column-major Mat4 with translate, scale, axis-angle
rotate and perspective, and a Quaternion with a Hamilton product and matrix
conversion; each frame the X, Y and Z rotations are composed as quaternions and
converted once. A ShaderProgram wrapper compiles stages on load, links lazily,
caches uniform locations and throws exceptions carrying the driver's info log.
There are two shader pairs: a flat colour derived from world position, and a
texture sampled through a planar projection of world yz, so files without UVs
still get textured.

Space cycles colour, texture, points and wireframe; WASD/RF rotate, arrows and
brackets translate, and after five idle seconds the model returns to auto-
rotation. It is about 1,200 lines in 14 C++ files plus 4 GLSL shaders, built
with a Makefile that links -lglfw -lGLEW -lGL on Linux.

Limitations: the texture path is hard-coded, only uncompressed 24-bit BMPs
without row padding are handled, normals, UVs and materials are ignored, there
is no lighting, and there are no automated tests.
